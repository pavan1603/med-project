DISEASE: diabetes
SOURCE: medical_guidelines
KEYWORDS: diabetes, blood sugar, glucose, insulin, pancreas, type 1, type 2, prediabetes

Diabetes is a chronic health condition that happens when the pancreas does not produce enough insulin or when the body cannot use insulin effectively.
Insulin helps move glucose from the blood into body cells for energy.
Common types include Type 1 diabetes, Type 2 diabetes, prediabetes and gestational diabetes.
Type 2 diabetes is the most common form and is often linked with insulin resistance.
Type 1 diabetes happens when the immune system damages insulin-producing cells in the pancreas.
Prediabetes means blood sugar is higher than normal but not yet in the diabetes range.
Diabetes should be confirmed with medical testing such as fasting glucose, HbA1c or oral glucose tolerance test.

TOPIC: glucose_levels
DISEASE: diabetes
SOURCE: medical_guidelines
KEYWORDS: glucose, blood sugar, mg/dL, fasting, threshold, HbA1c

Healthy blood glucose guidance:
Normal fasting glucose is usually below 100 mg/dL.
Prediabetes fasting glucose is usually 100 to 125 mg/dL.
Fasting glucose of 126 mg/dL or higher is in the diabetes range and should be confirmed with repeat or additional medical testing.
Normal 2-hour post-meal glucose is usually below 140 mg/dL.
Post-meal glucose of 200 mg/dL or higher may suggest diabetes and needs medical evaluation.
HbA1c below 5.7% is usually normal.
HbA1c 5.7% to 6.4% suggests prediabetes.
HbA1c 6.5% or higher suggests diabetes and should be confirmed by a qualified clinician.

TOPIC: diabetes_causes_simple
DISEASE: diabetes
SOURCE: medical_guidelines
KEYWORDS: reasons for diabetes, causes of diabetes, why diabetes, insulin resistance, family history

Diabetes can happen for different reasons depending on the type.
Type 2 diabetes is commonly linked to insulin resistance, excess body weight, low physical activity, family history, aging and unhealthy eating patterns.
Type 1 diabetes happens when the immune system attacks insulin-producing cells in the pancreas. It is not caused by eating sugar.
Prediabetes happens when blood sugar is higher than normal but not yet in the diabetes range.
Gestational diabetes can happen during pregnancy because of hormonal changes.
Some medicines, pancreatic disease, hormone problems, long-term stress and poor sleep can also affect blood sugar.
Eating too much sugar alone does not directly cause diabetes, but sugary drinks, refined carbohydrates and weight gain can increase risk over time.

TOPIC: bmi_obesity
DISEASE: diabetes
SOURCE: medical_guidelines
KEYWORDS: BMI, obesity, weight, body mass index, insulin resistance

BMI and body weight can affect diabetes risk.
BMI below 18.5 is underweight.
BMI 18.5 to 24.9 is usually considered a healthier range.
BMI 25 to 29.9 is overweight and may increase diabetes risk.
BMI 30 or higher is obesity and can increase insulin resistance.
Higher body weight, especially around the waist, can make it harder for insulin to work well.
Even modest weight loss can improve insulin sensitivity for many people with overweight or obesity.
BMI is only a screening measure and should be interpreted with age, muscle mass, waist size and medical history.

TOPIC: age_family_history_risk
DISEASE: diabetes
SOURCE: medical_guidelines
KEYWORDS: age, family history, diabetes pedigree, risk factors

Diabetes risk can increase with age, especially after 40 to 45 years.
Family history can increase risk, especially if a parent or sibling has Type 2 diabetes.
South Asian people can develop Type 2 diabetes at lower BMI compared with some other populations.
A history of gestational diabetes, polycystic ovary syndrome, high blood pressure or abnormal cholesterol can also increase risk.
Risk factors do not confirm diabetes, but they show who should be screened more carefully.
Regular screening is useful when risk factors are present.

SOURCE: medical_guidelines
KEYWORDS: risk factors, glucose, BMI, age, insulin, blood pressure, lifestyle

Important diabetes risk factors include:
High fasting or post-meal glucose.
HbA1c in the prediabetes or diabetes range.
Overweight or obesity, especially abdominal obesity.
Low physical activity.
Family history of diabetes.
Increasing age.
History of gestational diabetes or PCOS.
High blood pressure or abnormal cholesterol.
Smoking, poor sleep and chronic stress may also worsen metabolic health.
These risk factors should be used for screening and guidance, not for self-diagnosis.

KEYWORDS: symptoms, signs, warning, urination, thirst, fatigue, blurred vision

Warning signs and symptoms of diabetes can include:
Frequent urination, especially at night.
Excessive thirst and dry mouth.
Unexplained weight loss.
Extreme fatigue or weakness.
Blurred vision.
Slow healing cuts, wounds or bruises.
Frequent infections.
Tingling or numbness in hands and feet.
Dark patches of skin around the neck or armpits.
Some people have diabetes without clear symptoms, so testing is important when risk factors are present.

DISEASE: diabetes
SOURCE: medical_guidelines
KEYWORDS: prevention, lifestyle, diet, exercise, healthy weight, risk reduction

Diabetes prevention focuses on steady lifestyle habits.
Maintain a healthy weight or work toward gradual weight loss if overweight.
Aim for at least 150 minutes of moderate physical activity per week when medically safe.
Choose vegetables, legumes, whole grains, fruits in controlled portions and lean proteins.
Limit sugary drinks, sweets, refined carbohydrates and deep-fried foods.
Avoid smoking.
Sleep well and manage stress.
Check blood glucose regularly if you are above 40 or have risk factors.
Prediabetes can often improve with lifestyle changes and follow-up care.

[CHUNK_START]
TOPIC: diabetes_when_to_see_doctor
DISEASE: diabetes
SOURCE: medical_guidelines
KEYWORDS: doctor, testing, fasting glucose, HbA1c, emergency, high sugar

See a qualified doctor for confirmatory testing if fasting glucose is 126 mg/dL or higher, HbA1c is 6.5% or higher, or random glucose is very high with symptoms.
Ask for medical advice if you have excessive thirst, frequent urination, unexplained weight loss, blurred vision, fatigue or slow healing wounds.
Seek urgent care if very high blood sugar occurs with vomiting, confusion, severe weakness, dehydration, deep breathing or drowsiness.
Do not start, stop or change diabetes medicines without medical guidance.
MediRisk can support education and risk awareness, but it cannot diagnose diabetes.
