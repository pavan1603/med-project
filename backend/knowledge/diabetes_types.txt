Prediabetes medical guidelines:
Prediabetes is diagnosed when fasting glucose is 100 to 125 mg/dL.
It can often improve or return to the normal range with proper lifestyle changes.
Losing 5 to 7 percent of body weight reduces risk significantly.
Exercise 150 minutes per week to improve insulin sensitivity.
Reduce sugar and refined carbohydrate intake.
Regular glucose monitoring every 3 to 6 months recommended.
Without intervention 15 to 30 percent of prediabetics develop Type 2 within 5 years.

Type 1 diabetes medical guidelines:
Type 1 occurs when immune system destroys insulin producing beta cells in pancreas.
Patients require insulin injections or insulin pump for life.
Cannot be prevented or cured currently.
Includes subtypes: Classic Type 1, LADA, Neonatal diabetes, Wolfram Syndrome, Wolcott-Rallison Syndrome and MODY.
LADA is a slow developing Type 1 that appears in adults aged 30 to 50.
MODY is caused by single gene mutation and runs in families.
Neonatal diabetes affects babies under 6 months old.
Continuous glucose monitoring is recommended for Type 1 patients.

Type 2 diabetes medical guidelines:
Type 2 occurs when body does not use insulin properly — insulin resistance.
Most common form of diabetes affecting 90 to 95 percent of cases worldwide.
Strongly linked to obesity, sedentary lifestyle and poor diet.
Includes subtypes: Classic Type 2, Gestational diabetes and Steroid-Induced diabetes.
Gestational diabetes occurs during pregnancy due to hormonal changes.
Steroid-Induced diabetes is caused by long term steroid medication use.
Treatment includes diet changes, exercise, oral medication and sometimes insulin.
Can be managed well, and glucose levels may improve significantly with lifestyle changes and medical care.

Type 3 diabetes medical guidelines:
Type 3 diabetes is caused by physical damage to the pancreas or related organs.
Includes CFRD — Cystic Fibrosis Related Diabetes caused by thick mucus damaging pancreas.
Includes Type 3c — Pancreatogenic diabetes caused by pancreatitis or pancreatic surgery.
Requires insulin therapy in most cases.
Also requires digestive enzyme replacement therapy.
Medical evaluation is important because Type 3c or pancreatogenic diabetes can involve pancreatic disease or organ damage.
It usually needs long-term medical management and should not be self-treated.
