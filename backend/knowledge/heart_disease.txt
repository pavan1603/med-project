DISEASE: heart_disease

KEYWORDS: heart disease, cardiac, cardiovascular, overview, arteries

Heart disease is a group of conditions that affect the heart and blood vessels.
Coronary artery disease happens when blood vessels supplying the heart become narrowed or blocked.
Common risk factors include high blood pressure, high cholesterol, diabetes, smoking, unhealthy diet, physical inactivity, obesity, age and family history.
Heart disease risk can often be reduced with healthy habits, medical screening and treatment when needed.
Chest pain, breathlessness, fainting or pain spreading to the arm, jaw, neck or back should be treated seriously.
MediRisk can support risk awareness, but heart disease diagnosis needs medical evaluation.

KEYWORDS: heart risk factors, high BP, cholesterol, diabetes, smoking, family history

Important heart disease risk factors include:
High blood pressure.
High LDL cholesterol or high total cholesterol.
Diabetes or high blood sugar.
Smoking or tobacco use.
Low physical activity.
Unhealthy diet high in salt, fried foods, processed foods or excess sugar.
Overweight or obesity.
Family history of heart disease.
Increasing age.
Long-term stress and poor sleep may also affect heart health.
Having risk factors does not confirm heart disease, but it means screening and prevention are important.

DISEASE: heart_disease
SOURCE: medical_guidelines
KEYWORDS: age, gender, male, female, heart disease risk

Heart disease risk generally increases with age.
Men often develop heart disease risk earlier than women, but women also have significant risk, especially after menopause.
Family history of early heart disease increases risk for both men and women.
Age and gender are only screening factors. Symptoms, blood pressure, cholesterol, diabetes, smoking and medical tests are also important.
Women may sometimes have less typical symptoms such as breathlessness, fatigue, nausea, back pain or jaw pain.

TOPIC: cholesterol_guidelines
DISEASE: heart_disease
SOURCE: medical_guidelines
KEYWORDS: cholesterol, normal, borderline, high, LDL, HDL, triglycerides

Cholesterol guidance:
Total cholesterol below 200 mg/dL is usually considered desirable.
Total cholesterol 200 to 239 mg/dL is borderline high.
Total cholesterol 240 mg/dL or higher is high.
LDL is often called bad cholesterol because high LDL can contribute to plaque buildup in arteries.
HDL is often called good cholesterol because higher HDL can be protective.
Triglycerides are another blood fat that can increase heart risk when high.
Cholesterol results should be reviewed with a doctor because targets may differ for people with diabetes, heart disease or high risk.
[CHUNK_END]

TOPIC: blood_pressure_guidelines
DISEASE: heart_disease
SOURCE: medical_guidelines
KEYWORDS: blood pressure, normal, high, hypertension, mmHg

Blood pressure guidance:
Normal blood pressure is usually below 120/80 mmHg.
Elevated blood pressure is 120 to 129 systolic with diastolic below 80.
High blood pressure Stage 1 is usually 130 to 139 systolic or 80 to 89 diastolic.
High blood pressure Stage 2 is usually 140 or higher systolic or 90 or higher diastolic.
Blood pressure above 180 systolic or 120 diastolic can be a crisis, especially with symptoms, and needs urgent medical care.
High blood pressure can damage artery walls and increase risk of heart disease, stroke and kidney disease.
[CHUNK_END]

TOPIC: blocked_vessels_explained
DISEASE: heart_disease
SOURCE: medical_guidelines
KEYWORDS: blocked vessels, arteries, coronary, blockage, angiography

Blocked heart vessels mean the arteries supplying the heart may be narrowed by plaque.
This can reduce blood flow to the heart muscle.
Blocked vessels may cause chest pain, breathlessness, fatigue or heart attack, but some people may have few symptoms.
Tests such as ECG, stress test, echocardiogram, CT coronary angiography or coronary angiography may be used depending on the case.
The number and severity of blocked vessels should be interpreted by a cardiologist.
AI screening cannot confirm blocked vessels.
[CHUNK_END]

TOPIC: exercise_chest_pain
DISEASE: heart_disease
SOURCE: medical_guidelines
KEYWORDS: exercise pain, angina, chest pain, physical activity, breathlessness

Chest pain, tightness, pressure or breathlessness during walking, climbing stairs or exercise can be a warning sign.
This may happen when the heart is not getting enough blood during activity.
Stop activity and rest if symptoms occur.
Seek urgent medical care if chest pain is severe, lasts more than a few minutes, occurs at rest, or comes with sweating, faintness, nausea or pain spreading to arm, jaw, neck or back.
Do not start strenuous exercise if you have chest pain or unexplained breathlessness until a doctor clears you.

DISEASE: heart_disease
SOURCE: medical_guidelines
KEYWORDS: symptoms, warning signs, chest pain, shortness of breath, emergency

Warning signs of heart disease or heart attack can include:
Chest pain, pressure, heaviness, squeezing or tightness.
Shortness of breath at rest or with mild activity.
Pain spreading to the arm, jaw, neck, shoulder or back.
Dizziness, fainting or sudden weakness.
Irregular heartbeat or palpitations.
Swelling in legs, ankles or feet.
Unusual fatigue with minimal effort.
Cold sweats, nausea or vomiting without clear cause.
Seek emergency medical help immediately if chest pain or severe breathlessness occurs.
[CHUNK_END]

SOURCE: medical_guidelines
KEYWORDS: ECG, echo, stress test, cholesterol, troponin, angiography

Common heart tests include:
Blood pressure measurement to check hypertension.
Cholesterol and triglyceride tests to assess blood fats.
Blood sugar or HbA1c because diabetes increases heart risk.
ECG to check heart rhythm and possible signs of heart strain or heart attack.
Echocardiogram to assess heart pumping and valves.
Stress test to check how the heart responds to activity.
Troponin blood test when heart attack is suspected.
Angiography or CT coronary angiography to look for narrowed or blocked arteries.
A doctor chooses tests based on symptoms, risk factors and examination.
[CHUNK_END]

DISEASE: heart_disease
SOURCE: medical_guidelines
KEYWORDS: prevention, lifestyle, diet, exercise, healthy heart, smoking

Heart disease prevention tips:
Keep blood pressure under control.
Manage cholesterol with lifestyle and medicines when prescribed.
Control diabetes and blood sugar.
Avoid smoking and tobacco completely.
Aim for at least 150 minutes of moderate activity per week when medically safe.
Eat more vegetables, fruits, legumes, whole grains and lean proteins.
Limit salt, fried foods, processed foods, sugary drinks and excess alcohol.
Maintain a healthy weight.
Sleep well and manage stress.
Get regular checkups, especially if you are above 40 or have risk factors.

MediRisk may use a trained model to support heart disease risk screening.
Model output should be treated as risk guidance, not a final diagnosis.
A high-risk result means the user should consider medical evaluation, especially if symptoms or risk factors are present.
A low-risk result does not rule out heart disease if symptoms are present.
Final diagnosis requires doctor evaluation and appropriate tests.
