FOODS THAT SUPPORT DIABETES MANAGEMENT:
Food choices can help keep blood sugar steadier.
Choose foods that release glucose slowly, provide fiber and support fullness.
Helpful choices include non-starchy vegetables, legumes, whole grains in controlled portions, nuts and seeds in small amounts, low-fat curd and lean proteins.
Avoid very large meals because large portions of even healthy carbohydrates can raise blood sugar.
Try to eat balanced meals at regular times.

SUPPORTIVE FOODS AND SPICES FOR DIABETES:
Some traditional Indian foods and spices can be part of a diabetes-friendly eating pattern, but they should not be used as a cure or replacement for medicines.
Bitter gourd or karela can be included as a low-calorie vegetable.
Fenugreek or methi seeds contain soluble fiber and may help slow carbohydrate absorption when used in small food amounts.
Cinnamon can add flavor without sugar, but it should not be treated as diabetes medicine.
Turmeric can be used in normal cooking amounts as part of a balanced diet.
Amla and moringa leaves are nutritious foods that can be included in meals.
Diabetes management needs diet, physical activity, monitoring and medical care when required.

VEGETABLES FOR DIABETES:
Non-starchy vegetables are strongly recommended because they provide fiber, vitamins and minerals with relatively low calories.
Good choices include spinach, methi leaves, cabbage, cauliflower, broccoli, tomatoes, capsicum, cucumber, bottle gourd, bitter gourd, okra and beans.
These foods can improve meal quality and may reduce post-meal glucose rise when they replace refined carbohydrates.
Starchy vegetables such as potato, sweet potato, tapioca and raw banana can raise blood sugar more, so portions should be controlled.
Use steaming, boiling, grilling or light sauteing instead of deep frying.

FRUITS FOR DIABETES:
People with diabetes can usually eat whole fruits in controlled portions as part of a balanced meal plan.
Better choices include guava, apple, pear, papaya, berries, citrus fruits, pomegranate and amla.
Prefer whole fruit instead of fruit juice because whole fruit contains fiber and is more filling.
Higher-sugar fruits such as mango, banana, grapes, chikoo, jackfruit and dates are not automatically forbidden, but portions should be smaller and eaten less often depending on blood glucose response.
Avoid fruit juices, sweetened fruit drinks and large fruit portions because they can raise blood sugar quickly.
Pairing fruit with nuts, curd or a meal may reduce rapid glucose rise for some people.

HOW FRUITS MAY HELP REDUCE DIABETES RISK:
Whole fruits can support diabetes prevention when they replace sweets, biscuits, fruit juice or sugary snacks.
Fruit fiber slows digestion and may reduce sudden glucose spikes.
Fruits provide water, vitamins, minerals and antioxidants that support overall metabolic health.
Better choices include guava, apple, pear, papaya, berries, citrus fruits, pomegranate and amla.
Fruit juice is not preferred because it is easier to consume too much sugar quickly.
Fruits help as part of the full diet; they do not cure diabetes by themselves.

GRAINS AND PROTEINS FOR DIABETES:
Whole grains and millets can be used in controlled portions.
Better grain choices include whole-wheat roti, brown rice, oats, barley, jowar, bajra and ragi.
Millets and ragi can be helpful because they provide fiber, but portion size still matters.
Dal, chana, rajma, sprouts and other legumes provide protein and fiber.
Eggs, fish, lean chicken, tofu, paneer in controlled portions and low-fat curd can support protein intake when suitable.
Avoid making the meal mostly rice, roti or other starch. Add vegetables and protein to balance the plate.

RICE AND ROTI GUIDANCE FOR DIABETES:
A diabetes patient does not always need to completely stop rice or roti, but portions matter.
Prefer whole-wheat roti, millet roti or brown rice over maida items and large white rice portions.
A practical plate can include 1 to 2 small rotis or about half cup cooked rice, plus dal, vegetables and salad.
Avoid having rice, roti, potato and sweets together in the same meal.
White rice can raise blood sugar faster, so use smaller portions and pair it with dal, vegetables and curd.
Check blood sugar response because tolerance differs from person to person.

KEY NUTRIENTS FOR DIABETES MANAGEMENT:
Fiber from vegetables, fruits, legumes and whole grains slows sugar absorption and supports fullness.
Magnesium from nuts, seeds, legumes and leafy greens supports metabolic health.
Vitamin D deficiency is common and should be checked when advised by a doctor.
Vitamin B12 may need monitoring in some people, especially those taking metformin for a long time.
Omega-3 fats from fish, flaxseeds and walnuts can support heart health.
Nutrients should come mainly from food; supplements should be used only when advised by a qualified clinician.

FOODS THAT SUPPORT HEART HEALTH:
A heart-healthy diet can help manage cholesterol, blood pressure, weight and inflammation.
Helpful patterns include DASH-style and Mediterranean-style eating.
Eat more vegetables, fruits, legumes, whole grains, nuts and seeds in sensible portions.
Use healthier oils in small amounts instead of ghee, butter, vanaspati and deep-fried foods.
Limit salt, sugary drinks, sweets, processed foods, trans fats and excess saturated fat.

SUPPORTIVE FOODS FOR HEART HEALTH:
No single food can prevent or cure heart disease, but a healthy eating pattern can reduce risk.
Fatty fish such as sardines, salmon and mackerel provide omega-3 fats and can be included when available and suitable.
Walnuts, almonds and flaxseeds provide healthy fats and fiber, but portions should be small because nuts are calorie dense.
Garlic, turmeric and ginger can be used in normal cooking amounts for flavor, but they should not replace medicines for blood pressure, cholesterol or heart disease.
Olive, mustard, groundnut or sesame oil can be used in limited amounts instead of ghee, butter or vanaspati.
The most important pattern is more vegetables, fruits, legumes and whole grains, with less salt, sugar, saturated fat and trans fat.

VEGETABLES AND FRUITS FOR HEART HEALTH:
Vegetables and fruits support heart health because they provide fiber, potassium, vitamins and antioxidants.
Good choices include leafy greens, beans, carrots, bottle gourd, tomatoes, cucumber, citrus fruits, apple, papaya, pomegranate, berries and amla.
Replacing fried snacks and sweets with fruit or vegetables can improve overall diet quality.
Potassium-rich foods may support blood pressure control for many people, but people with kidney disease should follow medical advice.
Use fruits in sensible portions and avoid fruit juices with added sugar.

FOODS TO LIMIT FOR DIABETES AND HEART HEALTH:
Sugary drinks and sodas can raise blood sugar quickly and add excess calories.
Large portions of white rice, white bread and maida foods can raise blood sugar faster.
Deep-fried foods can add unhealthy fats and excess calories.
Processed meats and packaged snacks are often high in salt and preservatives.
Full-fat dairy, butter, ghee and cream should be limited, especially for heart risk.
Sweets, cakes, pastries and biscuits should be occasional and small if used at all.
Excess salt can raise blood pressure.
Alcohol can affect blood pressure, triglycerides, blood sugar and medicines, so follow medical advice.
