WHY DIET IS IMPORTANT FOR HEART HEALTH:
A heart-friendly diet can help manage cholesterol, blood pressure, body weight and blood sugar.
Eating too much salt, fried food, processed food, sugary drinks and unhealthy fat can increase heart risk over time.
A healthy eating pattern can reduce the chance of plaque buildup and support better blood vessel health.
Diet works best along with physical activity, sleep, stress management, tobacco avoidance and medicines when prescribed.
Diet should not replace medical treatment for heart disease.

HEART HEALTHY FOODS IN INDIAN DIET:
Dals and legumes such as moong dal, masoor dal, toor dal, chana and rajma provide fiber and protein.
Whole grains such as brown rice, ragi, jowar, bajra, whole-wheat roti and oats can support cholesterol and blood pressure control when portions are sensible.
Leafy greens such as palak, methi and cabbage provide fiber, potassium and micronutrients.
Vegetables should fill a large part of the plate. Steam, boil or lightly saute instead of deep frying.
Fruits such as papaya, apple, orange, pomegranate and amla can be included in sensible portions.
Fermented foods such as idli, dosa, dhokla and buttermilk can be included when prepared with less oil and salt.
Healthy oils such as mustard, groundnut, sesame or olive oil should still be used in small amounts.
Nuts and seeds can be used as a small handful, not unlimited portions.

SOUTH INDIAN HEART PATIENT DIET PLAN:
Breakfast: Idli with sambar using less oil, or vegetable oats upma.
Mid-morning: Fresh fruit or sundal.
Lunch: Brown rice in controlled portion with rasam, poriyal and salad, or ragi dosa with vegetable kootu.
Evening snack: Roasted chana, sprouts or buttermilk without added salt.
Dinner: Ragi dosa with vegetable kootu, or millet khichdi with curd and light vegetable curry.
Use minimal oil in cooking.
Limit ghee roast, excess chutney, fried snacks and salty packaged foods.

NORTH INDIAN HEART PATIENT DIET PLAN:
Breakfast: Vegetable poha, oats upma or moong dal chilla.
Mid-morning: Fresh fruit like papaya, apple or pomegranate.
Lunch: 2 rotis with dal, sabzi and salad.
Evening snack: Roasted chana, sprouts, buttermilk or fruit in controlled portion.
Dinner: Millet khichdi with curd and light vegetable curry, or roti with dal and sabzi.
Use whole-wheat roti instead of maida.
Choose brown rice or millets in controlled portions.
Use less oil and avoid cream-based gravies like dal makhani with butter.

HEART DIET VEGETARIAN AND NON-VEGETARIAN OPTIONS:
Breakfast vegetarian: 2 steamed idlis with mint chutney, vegetable oats upma, poha with vegetables or moong dal chilla.

Mid-morning non-vegetarian: Clear chicken soup without cream or excess salt.
Lunch vegetarian: Brown rice or roti with moong dal, vegetable sambar, beans poriyal, salad and low-fat curd.
Lunch non-vegetarian: Grilled fish or skinless chicken with vegetables and a small portion of brown rice or roti.
Use little oil and avoid deep frying.

FOODS TO LIMIT FOR HEART PATIENTS:
Deep-fried snacks such as vadas, pakoras and samosas can add unhealthy fats and excess calories.
Excess ghee, butter, vanaspati and cream can increase saturated fat intake.
Sweets such as jalebi, mysore pak and payasam are high in sugar and calories.
Maida items such as white bread and bakery snacks offer little fiber.
Packaged junk foods are often high in salt and preservatives.
Processed meats can be high in sodium and unhealthy fats.
Sugary drinks and sodas can increase calories and triglycerides.
Choose fresh home-cooked meals with less oil and salt whenever possible.

DAILY HABITS FOR HEART HEALTH:
Walk or do moderate activity regularly when medically safe.
Aim for 150 minutes of moderate physical activity per week if cleared by a doctor.
Sleep 7 to 8 hours when possible because poor sleep can affect blood pressure and metabolism.
Practice yoga, breathing exercises or relaxation techniques to reduce stress.
Avoid smoking and tobacco completely.
Limit alcohol according to medical advice.
Check blood pressure and cholesterol regularly.
If you have diabetes, high blood pressure, high cholesterol or previous heart disease, follow a personalized plan from a clinician.
Small and consistent changes in diet and lifestyle can make a meaningful difference over time.
